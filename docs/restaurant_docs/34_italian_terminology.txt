LA BELLA TAVOLA - ITALIAN MENU TERMINOLOGY GUIDE

COOKING METHODS & PREPARATIONS

Al Forno
- English: "In the oven" or "baked"
- Preparation: Slow-roasted or baked in oven
- Example: Branzino al Forno (baked whole fish)
- Timing: Usually longer cooking, develops flavor

Alla Griglia
- English: "On the grill" or "grilled"
- Preparation: Cooked on open flame or grill
- Example: Costolette di Agnello alla Griglia (grilled lamb chops)
- Kitchen: Open-flame charring, smoky flavor

Fritto/Fritta
- English: "Fried" or "deep-fried"
- Preparation: Cooked in hot oil
- Example: Calamari Fritti (fried squid)
- Temperature: Oil heated to proper hot temperature

In Umido
- English: "Stewed" or "braised"
- Preparation: Slow-cooked in liquid
- Example: Pappardelle in Umido (braised pasta dish)
- Timing: Long, slow cooking develops depth

Affumicato
- English: "Smoked"
- Preparation: Smoked preparation method
- Example: Salmon Affumicato
- Flavor: Distinctive smoky taste

Ripieno/Ripiena
- English: "Stuffed" or "filled"
- Preparation: Filled with ingredients
- Example: Calamari Ripieno (stuffed squid)
- Complexity: Multiple ingredient layers

SAUCE TERMS & DESCRIPTIONS

Ragù
- English: "Sauce" or specifically "meat sauce"
- Origin: Emilia-Romagna region
- Base: Slow-cooked meat with tomatoes
- Timing: Several hours of cooking
- Famous Variation: Ragù Bolognese

Sugo
- English: "Sauce" (general term)
- Usage: Any savory sauce
- Common Types: Tomato, cream, meat-based
- Versatility: Various regional variations

Marinara
- English: "Sailor style" or tomato sauce
- Ingredients: Tomatoes, garlic, basil, oil
- Simplicity: Minimal ingredients
- Clarity: Clear tomato flavor

Pesto
- English: "Pounded" (preparation method)
- Primary: Basil-based sauce
- Ingredients: Basil, pine nuts, garlic, cheese, oil
- Regional: Originates from Genoa, Liguria

TEXTURE & INGREDIENT TERMS

Al Dente
- English: "To the tooth"
- Meaning: Pasta cooked until barely tender, still firm
- Preference: Italian standard for pasta texture
- Mouth feel: Slightly resistant texture
- Explanation: Allows sauce absorption

Morbido
- English: "Soft" or "tender"
- Meaning: Meat fully cooked, very tender
- Usage: Braised meats, slow-cooked preparations
- Time: Longer cooking time required
- Texture: Falls apart easily

Croccante
- English: "Crispy" or "crunchy"
- Meaning: Crispy texture maintained
- Usage: Fried items, texture contrast
- Example: Calamari Fritti (crispy exterior)
- Preservation: Served immediately for crispness

Vellutata
- English: "Velvety" or "smooth"
- Meaning: Smooth, creamy sauce or soup
- Preparation: Blended or strained smooth
- Texture: Silk-like mouthfeel
- Example: Mushroom Vellutata (creamy mushroom soup)

MEAT & PROTEIN TERMS

Vitello
- English: "Veal" (calf meat)
- Age: Meat from very young cattle
- Flavor: Mild, delicate
- Preparation: Often thin-sliced and quick-cooked
- Example: Saltimbocca di Vitello

Agnello
- English: "Lamb"
- Age: Young sheep meat
- Flavor: Rich, distinctive
- Tenderness: Very tender, delicate
- Example: Costolette di Agnello (lamb chops)

Manzo/Manzo Braisato
- English: "Beef" or "braised beef"
- Cut: Often chuck or brisket
- Preparation: Slow-braised
- Tenderness: Becomes very tender with braising
- Example: Branzata di Manzo

Maiale
- English: "Pork"
- Varieties: Multiple muscle and part preparations
- Flavor: Rich, well-flavored
- Tradition: Important in Italian cuisine
- Example: Arrosto di Maiale (roasted pork)

Selvaggina
- English: "Game meat" or "wild meat"
- Examples: Cinghiale (wild boar), Lepre (hare)
- Flavor: Distinctive, rich
- Seasonality: Fall and winter hunting season
- Rarity: Special occasion dishes

Branzino/Branzini
- English: "Sea bass" or "Mediterranean sea bass"
- Type: Whole fish typically
- Flavor: Delicate, sweet
- Preparation: Often whole roasted
- Sustainability: Generally good choice

Pese Spada
- English: "Swordfish"
- Cuts: Thick steaks, meaty
- Flavor: Rich, firm-textured
- Preparation: Grilled or pan-seared
- Sustainability: Check with server

Gamberi/Gamberoni
- English: "Shrimp" or "jumbo prawns"
- Size: Gamberi = smaller, Gamberoni = larger
- Flavor: Sweet, tender
- Cooking: Quick-cooking (few minutes)

VEGETABLE & INGREDIENT TERMINOLOGY

Melanzane
- English: "Eggplant"
- Preparation: Usually grilled, fried, or baked
- Flavor: Mild, absorbs flavors well
- Famous Dish: Melanzane alla Parmigiana

Zucchine
- English: "Zucchini" (Italian squash)
- Season: Summer vegetable
- Preparation: Grilled, sautéed
- Flavor: Mild, slightly sweet

Pomodori
- English: "Tomatoes"
- Varieties: San Marzano famous
- Season: Summer peak
- Preparation: Raw, cooked, stewed

Funghi/Porcini
- English: "Mushrooms" / "Porcini" (specific type)
- Porcini: Dry usually available (imported)
- Flavor: Earthy, rich
- Usage: Sauces, risotto, broths

Aglio
- English: "Garlic"
- Usage: Foundational ingredient
- Preparation: Sliced, minced, whole
- Flavor: Building block of many dishes

COOKING VESSEL & PRESENTATION TERMS

Teglia
- English: "Baking pan" or "dish prepared in pan"
- Usage: Pan-roasted or baked items
- Presentation: Served in pan or on platter
- Informality: Suggests rustic preparation

Piatto/Piattino
- English: "Plate" / "small plate"
- Piatto: Regular dinner plate
- Piattino: Appetizer/side plate

Zuppiera
- English: "Soup tureen" or "serving bowl"
- Usage: Soup service or family-style
- Presentation: Impressive shared service

COOKING TIME & TEMPERATURE TERMS

Cotto
- English: "Cooked" (general)
- Meaning: Fully cooked through
- Usage: Any doneness level described

Semibreve
- English: "Medium rare"
- Beef: Pink center
- Timing: Still warm center
- Preference: European standard for steak

Bene Cotto
- English: "Well done"
- Meaning: Fully cooked, no pink
- Beef: Grey throughout
- Request: Common preference

DESCRIPTIVE TASTING TERMS

Fresco
- English: "Fresh"
- Meaning: Recently prepared, bright flavors
- Usage: Describes ingredient or preparation
- Example: Pomodori Freschi (fresh tomatoes)

Delicato
- English: "Delicate"
- Meaning: Subtle flavors, light profile
- Example: Branzino (delicate fish)

Robusto
- English: "Robust" or "bold"
- Meaning: Strong, assertive flavors
- Example: Ragù Bolognese (robust sauce)

Elegante
- English: "Elegant"
- Meaning: Refined, sophisticated preparation
- Usage: Description of plating or combination

PORTION & MENU STRUCTURE TERMS

Antipasto/Antipasti
- English: "Before the main" or appetizers
- Course: First course
- Number: Antipasti (plural)
- Usually: Shared or individual

Primo
- English: "First course"
- Meaning: Pasta, risotto, or soup course
- Italy: Central to meal

Secondo
- English: "Second course" or main course
- Meaning: Protein course
- Italy: Meat or fish course after primo

Contorno
- English: "Side dish" or "vegetable course"
- Traditional: Served separately from protein
- Variety: Various vegetable preparations

Dolce
- English: "Sweet" or "dessert"
- Course: Final course of meal
- Examples: Tiramisu, Panna Cotta, Gelato

SPECIAL PREPARATION NOTES

Al Tartufo
- English: "With truffle"
- Ingredient: Black or white truffle
- Luxury: Premium ingredient
- Flavor: Distinctive, earthy richness

Con Panna
- English: "With cream"
- Meaning: Sauce contains cream
- Richness: Creamy, luxurious texture

All'Olio
- English: "With oil"
- Meaning: Oil-based sauce
- Richness: Lighter than cream-based
- Flavor: Olive oil forward

Al Burro
- English: "With butter"
- Meaning: Butter-based sauce
- Richness: Rich, luxurious
- Context: Often pun for pasta, light for fish

Piccata
- English: "Thin sliced with lemon sauce"
- Preparation: Thin-sliced meat
- Sauce: Lemon and caper sauce
- Quickness: Quick cooking method
