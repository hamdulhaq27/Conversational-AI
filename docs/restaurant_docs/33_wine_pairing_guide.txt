LA BELLA TAVOLA - WINE PAIRING GUIDE

WINE PAIRING FUNDAMENTALS

Philosophy:
- Enhance food through complementary wines
- Personal preference always respected
- Guidelines provided, not requirements
- Experimentation encouraged
- Sommelier consultation available

Pairing Goals:
- Wine and food balance each other
- Neither overwhelms the other
- Flavors enhanced through combination
- Memorable dining experience created
- Personal enjoyment prioritized

Basic Pairing Principles:
- Lighter wines with lighter food
- Fuller wines with heavier food
- Matching regional wines with regional food
- Acid cutting through rich foods
- Fruit notes echoing food flavors

PAIRING BY COURSE

Aperitivo & Appetizers

Champagne & Sparkling:
- Universally appetizing
- Brightens palate
- Celebratory feel
- Prosecco, Franciacorta recommended

White Wine:
- Crisp and refreshing
- Gavi, Vermentino excellent
- Pinot Grigio accessible
- Greco di Tufo complex

Red Wine Options:
- Light reds possible
- Chilled red option
- Amaro as aperitivo alternative

Antipaste Specific:
- Burrata: Vermentino, Gavi
- Carpaccio: Light red or white
- Calamari Fritti: Crisp white
- Prosciutto: Prosecco, light white

FIRST COURSE (PASTA) PAIRINGS

Tomato-Based Sauces:
- Chianti Classico classic pairing
- Barbera d'Alba excellent
- Vino Nobile di Montepulciano
- Red wine acidity cuts tomato richness

Cream-Based Sauces:
- White wine generally preferred
- Gavi or similar crisp white
- Oak-aged white optional for richness
- Avoid very dry, very acidic whites

Meat Ragù:
- Full-bodied red wine needed
- Barolo or Barbaresco excellent
- Brunello di Montalcino premium
- Wine can age 30+ minutes for softening

Seafood Pasta:
- White wine typical pairing
- Light to medium-bodied white
- Vermentino, Albariño work well
- Avoid very oaky whites

Simple Preparations:
- Lighter wines preferred
- Aglioli or al Olio needs bright white
- Spaghetti al Pomodoro versatile
- Barbera d'Alba surprisingly good

MAIN COURSE PAIRINGS

Fish & Seafood Mains:
- White wine generally preferred
- Gavi, Vermentino, Albariño
- Some full whites possible
- Avoid tannin-heavy reds

Lighter Fish Preparation:
- Crisp, cool white preferred
- Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc
- Sparkling works well
- Avoid oak and tannin

Rich Fish Preparation:
- Full-bodied white acceptable
- Greco di Tufo, Chardonnay styles
- Light red with some fish
- Salmon pairs with light reds

Poultry (Chicken/Duck):
- Red or white wine both work
- Medium to medium-full body needed
- Duck: Medium reds preferred
- Chicken: Light reds or whites
- Barbera d'Alba excellent

Light Meat Preparation:
- Lighter reds work well
- Vino Nobile di Montepulciano
- Chianti Classico good choice
- Barbera d'Alba accessible

Heavy Meat Preparation:
- Full-bodied reds essential
- Barolo, Brunello primary choices
- Wine can be younger for heat
- Age softens tannins

Veal Specialties:
- Saltimbocca: Chianti Classico
- Osso Buco: Barolo essential
- Piccata: Lighter reds or whites
- Temperature affects wine choice

Braised Preparations:
- Full-bodied red essential
- Wine used in braise complements
- Barolo, Brunello appropriate
- Kitchen coordination possible

WINE STORAGE & CONDITION

Proper Serving Temperature:
- Whites: 45-55°F ideally
- Reds: 60-65°F ideally
- Room temperature acceptable for reds
- Chilled whites refreshing
- Restaurant maintains proper temps

Bottle Condition Concerns:
- Cork taint issues possible (rare)
- Return if wine tastes off
- Restaurant takes quality seriously
- Replacement offered for questionable bottles
- No pressure or judgment

Glassware Impact:
- Proper wine glass enhances experience
- Not necessary for enjoyment
- Proper glasses provided by restaurant
- Affects aroma perception
- Temperature maintenance better in proper glass

FREQUENTLY ASKED PAIRING QUESTIONS

What wine goes with pasta?
Typically red wine for meat sauce, white for cream or seafood sauce. Personal preference applies.

Should I get wine with lunch?
Absolutely if you'd like. Light wines work well. Wine by the glass available.

How much wine should I drink?
Whatever amount enhances your experience. No pressure to drink. Non-alcoholic options available.

Can I get a wine pairing that's not the suggestion?
Absolutely. Sommelier happy to suggest alternatives. Personal preference respected.

SOMMELIER CONSULTATION PROCESS

Requesting Sommelier:
- Ask server "Can I speak with the sommelier?"
- Sommelier comes to table
- Ten-minute consultation standard
- Food menu review together
- Wine preferences discussed

Information Shared:
- Describe flavor preferences
- Budget per bottle discussed
- Food selections shared
- Allergies or dislikes mentioned
- Number of people drinking

Recommendations Provided:
- 2-3 options suggested
- Tasting note descriptions
- Pairing reasoning explained
- Price points compared
- Personal preference honored

Selection & Service:
- Choose wine from options

- Tasting pour offered
