ITALIAN CULINARY REGIONS

Northern Italy (Alps to Po River):
- Butter and cream sauces predominant
- Risotto specialty (Piedmont, Lombardy)
- Meat and game important
- Fresh pasta common (egg pasta)
- Dairy and cheese central
- Wine culture strong (Barolo, Barbaresco)

Central Italy (Tuscany, Umbria):
- Tomato-based cuisine
- Olive oil emphasis (Tuscan oil famous)
- Game meats (wild boar)
- Hearty preparations
- Wine production important
- Pecorino Romano cheese
- Bread and grains featured

Southern Italy (Below Rome):
- Poverty historical → creativity in cooking
- Tomatoes, garlic, oil foundation
- Seafood and fish important
- Pasta more common than risotto
- Spice and heat more present
- Dried pasta tradition
- Different culinary identity

Sicily (Island):
- Unique fusion cuisine
- Arab, Norman, Spanish influences
- Seafood primary focus
- Raisins and pine nuts common
- Bold, complex flavors
- Arancini (fried rice balls) iconic
- Caponata (eggplant stew) famous

INGREDIENT IMPORTANCE

Olive Oil:
- Extra virgin for finishing dishes
- Regular for cooking
- Regional variations prized (Tuscan famous)
- Quality paramount
- Cost reflects quality

Tomatoes:
- San Marzano tomatoes (Campania)
- Peak season summer (June-August)
- Canned preserved (DOP certification)
- Foundational in Southern cooking
- Acidity and flavor essential

Garlic:
- Foundational ingredient
- Building block of many dishes
- Varied preparation methods
- Raw, cooked, roasted variations
- Central to Italian cooking

Herbs:
- Basil (Southern Italy)
- Oregano (Southern Italy)
- Rosemary (Tuscany)
- Thyme (various regions)
- Fresh preferred when possible
- Dried acceptable off-season

Cheese:
- Parmigiano-Reggiano (Northern)
- Pecorino Romano (Central/Southern)
- Mozzarella (Buffalo preferred)
- Regional cheeses celebrated
- Quality critical

MEAL STRUCTURE & TIMING

Traditional Italian Meal:

Aperitivo:
- Before-dinner drinks and light bites
- Prosecco or wine common
- Olives, nuts, light appetizers
- 30-60 minutes before meal

Antipasto:
- First course, small portions
- Cold items (cured meats, cheeses)
- Hot items (fried items, baked)
- Shared family-style traditional
- Sets tone for meal

Primo:
- First major course
- Pasta, risotto, or soup
- Central to Italian meal
- Smaller portion than "main"
- Satisfying but not final course

Secondo:
- Second/main course (confusingly)
- Protein-focused (meat, fish)
- Smaller portions than American mains
- Substantial but not overly heavy
- Complements primo

Contorno:
- Side dish course (vegetables)
- Traditionally served separately
- Simple preparations (grilled, sautéed)
- Rounds out meal nutritionally
- Cleansing effect before dessert

Dolce:
- Dessert course
- Sweet preparation
- Followed by coffee/digestivo

Caffè:
- Express and small coffee
- After dessert tradition
- Aids digestion
- Social continuation

Digestivo:
- After-dinner drink
- Amaro (herbal liqueur)
- Limoncello (lemon liqueur)
- Grappa (spirit)
- Aids digestion, friendly conclusion

MEAL TIMING CULTURE

Lunch (Pranzo):
- Main meal of day traditionally
- 1-2 hours duration
- Multiple courses leisurely paced
- Family gathering important
- 12-2 PM typical timing

Dinner (Cena):
- Lighter than lunch traditionally
- Becoming more substantial (modernization)
- 8-10 PM typical timing (later than American norm)
- Family time important
- Conversation emphasized

RESTAURANT CULTURE IN ITALY

Trattoria vs. Ristorante:
- Trattoria: Casual, family-run, simple
- Ristorante: Formal, refined, elegant
- La Bella Tavola: Trattoria-style warmth with ristorante quality
- Both serve authentic Italian cuisine
- Atmosphere differs, quality similar

Neighborhood Restaurants:
- Community gathering places
- Regular customers known by name
- Multi-generational clientele
- Lower prices than touristy areas
- Authentic preparation focus
