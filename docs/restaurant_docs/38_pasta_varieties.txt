LA BELLA TAVOLA - PASTA VARIETIES & REGIONAL SHAPES

PASTA BASICS

Definition:
- Italian pasta made from durum wheat semolina
- Traditional shapes tied to regions
- Shape affects sauce adherence
- Regional identities strong in Italy
- Each shape has culinary purpose

Fresh vs. Dried Pasta:
- Fresh pasta: Soft texture, quick cooking
- Dried pasta: Al dente texture preferred, longer cooking
- Restaurant uses both types
- Fresh for handmade preparations
- Dried Italian imports for classics

LONG PASTA VARIETIES

Spaghetti
- Round, thin strands
- Most recognizable pasta shape
- Pairs with light to medium sauces
- Oil-based and tomato sauces ideal
- Al dente texture essential

Linguine
- Flat, ribbon-like strands
- Slightly thicker than spaghetti
- Pairs with seafood beautifully
- Light sauces adhere well
- Restaurant specialty: Linguine alle Vongole

Fettuccine
- Wider, ribbon-like shape (1/4 inch)
- Pairs with cream sauces excellently
- Rich sauces coat well
- Alfredo traditional pairing
- Hearty, satisfying texture

Pappardelle
- Very wide ribbons (1 inch)
- Traditional Tuscan shape
- Pairs with meat ragù
- Robust, hearty sauces needed
- Restaurant favorite: Pappardelle al Cinghiale

Tagliatelle
- Medium ribbon width
- Traditional Bologna pasta
- Pairs with Bolognese sauce
- Regional specialty pairing
- Restaurant staple: Tagliatelle al Ragù

SHORT PASTA VARIETIES

Penne
- Tube shape, diagonal cut ends
- Versatile shape, many sauces
- Ridged surface holds sauce
- Hearty dishes appropriate
- Arrabbiata traditional pairing

Rigatoni
- Larger tube, ridged surface
- Holds chunky sauces well
- Baked pasta dishes (lasagna-type)
- Meat and cream sauces pair well
- Restaurant uses: Rigatoni alla Matriciana

Fusilli
- Spiral/twisted shape
- Holds sauce well
- Good with creamy sauces
- Vegetable preparations work well
- Comfort food associations

Orzo
- Rice-grain shaped
- Technically rice-shaped pasta
- Prepared risotto-style sometimes
- Lighter applications
- Creamy sauce pairings

SPECIALTY PASTA SHAPES

Ravioli
- Filled pasta pockets
- Traditionally meat or cheese-filled
- Restaurant uses: Lobster ravioli, spinach ricotta
- Butter or light sauce preferred
- Handmade in restaurant

Tortellini/Tortelloni
- Small filled pasta, folded
- Regional variations in size
- Tortellini: Small, typically filled meat
- Tortelloni: Larger, cheese or vegetable
- Broth or sauce served

Lasagna Sheets
- Large flat pasta sheets
- Layered with sauce and cheese
- Baked preparation
- Traditional family dish
- Restaurant specialty: Lasagna della Nonna

Agnolotti
- Small filled pasta pockets
- Folded triangle shape
- Elegant plating possible
- Delicate preparation
- Restaurant featured: Weekly Wednesday special

PASTA SELECTION GUIDE

Pairing Pasta with Sauce:

Light Oil-Based Sauces:
- Spaghetti or linguine
- Thin pasta allows sauce coating
- Al aglio e olio traditional
- Delicate flavors shine

Tomato-Based Sauces:
- Penne or spaghetti ideal
- Chunky or smooth both work
- Acidity of tomato important
- Marinara simple pairing

Cream Sauces:
- Fettuccine preferred
- Pappardelle also works
- Wider pasta holds cream
- Alfredo classic pairing

Meat Ragù:
- Pappardelle traditional
- Tagliatelle also traditional
- Wider pasta necessary
- Hearty sauce coating
- Long cooking sauce compatibility

Seafood:
- Linguine traditional
- Spaghetti also works
- Subtle flavors highlighted
- Delicate preparation needed
- Clam sauce natural pairing

PASTA COOKING TECHNIQUES

Proper Cooking:
- Abundant salted boiling water
- Al dente texture (slight firmness)
- Time varies by pasta type
- Fresh vs. dried cooking times differ
- Taste test for doneness

Sauce Integration:
- Drain pasta, reserve 1 cup pasta water
- Toss pasta with sauce
- Add pasta water if sauce too thick
- Emulsification improves coating
- Finish with cheese or oil

PASTA STORAGE & SHELF LIFE

Dried Pasta:
- Shelf stable for years
- Proper dry storage needed
- Room temperature ideal
- Airtight container preferred
- Check expiration rarely (long life)

Fresh Pasta:
- Refrigerate if not using immediately
- 2-3 days refrigerated (egg pasta)
- Freezing extends life (weeks)
- Quality best within 2-3 days
- Properly wrapped prevents drying

COOKING PASTA AT HOME

Tips from Restaurant:
- Use abundant salty water
- Don't break long pasta
- Stir occasionally to prevent sticking
- Test for doneness starting 1 minute before package time
- Reserve pasta water before draining
- Toss immediately with sauce

Common Mistakes:
- Not enough water
- Water not salty enough
- Breaking pasta unnecessarily
- Rinsing pasta (removes starch)
- Adding oil to water (unnecessary)
- Overcooking pasta
